## Random Variables

A **random variable** is a variable whose value is determined by the outcome of a random experiment. It assigns a numerical value to each outcome in the sample space.

Unlike standard variables, random variables take numerical values with associated probabilities.

<Flashcard front="What is a random variable?" back="A variable whose value is **determined by the outcome of a random experiment**, assigning a numerical value to each outcome with an associated probability." />

---

## Probability Distributions

A **probability distribution** is a mathematical model that describes all possible outcomes of a random variable along with their associated probabilities.

### Properties

1. All probabilities must be between 0 and 1: $0 \leq P(X = x) \leq 1$
2. The sum of all probabilities must equal 1: $\sum P(X = x) = 1$

### Classification

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Discrete** | Takes specific, countable values | Binomial, Poisson, Bernoulli |
| **Continuous** | Takes any value within an interval | Normal, Uniform, Exponential |

<Flashcard front="What are the two properties of a probability distribution?" back="1. All probabilities are between 0 and 1: $0 \\leq P(X = x) \\leq 1$\n2. The sum of all probabilities equals 1: $\\sum P(X = x) = 1$" />

## Mathematical Expectation

The **expected value** (or population mean) of a random variable $X$ is:

### For Discrete Random Variables

$$\mu = E(X) = \sum x \cdot P(x)$$

### For Continuous Random Variables

$$\mu = E(X) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x \cdot f(x) \, dx$$

### Variance

$$\sigma^2 = E(X^2) - [E(X)]^2$$

### Standard Deviation

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2}$$

<Flashcard front="How do you calculate the expected value of a discrete random variable?" back="$E(X) = \\sum x \\cdot P(x)$ — multiply each value by its probability and sum." />

<Flashcard front="What is the relationship between variance and standard deviation?" back="Variance $\\sigma^2$ is the **square** of standard deviation $\\sigma$, and standard deviation is the **square root** of variance: $\\sigma = \\sqrt{\\sigma^2}$." />

## Bernoulli Trials

A **Bernoulli trial** is a random experiment with exactly two outcomes:
- **Success** (with probability $p$)
- **Failure** (with probability $q = 1 - p$)

Key properties:
- Trials are **independent**
- The probability of success remains **constant** across trials

<Flashcard front="What are the properties of Bernoulli trials?" back="1. Exactly **two outcomes** (success/failure)\n2. Trials are **independent**\n3. Probability of success $p$ is **constant**" />

## Binomial Distribution

The binomial distribution counts the **number of successes** in $n$ independent Bernoulli trials.

### Parameters
- $n$ — number of trials
- $p$ — probability of success
- $q = 1 - p$ — probability of failure

### Probability Mass Function

$$P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k q^{n-k}$$

where $k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots, n$

### Properties

| Property | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Mean | $\mu = np$ |
| Variance | $\sigma^2 = npq$ |
| Standard Deviation | $\sigma = \sqrt{npq}$ |

<Flashcard front="What is the binomial distribution used for?" back="It counts the **number of successes** in $n$ independent Bernoulli trials, each with constant probability $p$ of success." />

<Flashcard front="What is the mean and variance of a binomial distribution?" back="Mean: $\\mu = np$. Variance: $\\sigma^2 = npq$ where $q = 1 - p$." />

## Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution models the **number of events** occurring in a fixed interval of time or space. It is a limiting form of the binomial distribution when $n$ is large and $p$ is small.

### Parameters

$\lambda$ — average number of occurrences in the interval

### Probability Mass Function

$$P(X = k) = \frac{\lambda^k e^{-\lambda}}{k!}$$

where $k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$

### Properties

| Property | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| Mean | $\mu = \lambda$ |
| Variance | $\sigma^2 = \lambda$ |

<Flashcard front="When do you use the Poisson distribution?" back="When counting the **number of events** in a fixed interval of time or space, especially when $n$ is large and $p$ is small." />

<Flashcard front="What is unique about the mean and variance of a Poisson distribution?" back="The mean and variance are **equal**: both equal $\\lambda$." />

## Normal Distribution

The **Normal (Gaussian) Distribution** is the most important distribution in statistics. It is symmetric and bell-shaped.

### Parameters

- $\mu$ — mean (center of the distribution)
- $\sigma$ — standard deviation (spread)

### Key Properties

- Mean = Median = Mode
- Symmetric about the mean
- Asymptotic (tails approach but never touch the x-axis)
- Total area under the curve = 1
- **Mesokurtic** (medium peak)

### The Empirical Rule

| Range | Percentage of Data |
|-------|-------------------|
| $\mu \pm 1\sigma$ | 68.3% |
| $\mu \pm 2\sigma$ | 95.4% |
| $\mu \pm 3\sigma$ | 99.7% |

### Z-Score

$$z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$

The Z-score measures how many standard deviations a data point is from the mean.

<Flashcard front="What is the Empirical Rule?" back="68.3% of data falls within $\\pm 1\\sigma$, 95.4% within $\\pm 2\\sigma$, and 99.7% within $\\pm 3\\sigma$ of the mean." />

<Flashcard front="What is a Z-score?" back="A Z-score measures how many **standard deviations** a data point is from the mean: $z = \\frac{x - \\mu}{\\sigma}$." />
